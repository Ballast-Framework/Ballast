# Known Gaps and Roadmap

Ballast v1.0.0 is the first public release. This page lists what v1.0 does not yet do, so that adopters and reviewers can see its limits before relying on it. The items come from the authors' own review of the framework before release. Targets are indicative and will change with feedback; nothing here is a committed date.

If you can help close any of these gaps, see [How to help](#how-to-help) below.

## Validation

- **Independent validation and pilots.** Ballast is practitioner-authored. Its control text, [Control Tiers](guides/tiering.md#control-tiers), capability tags and per-asset counts have not yet been independently reviewed or tested in assessments at multiple organizations. In particular, the testability rule (two assessors should reach the same pass or fail result) has not been measured. *Target: starting with v1.0.* We are looking for organizations willing to assess one or more AI assets against [T1](guides/tiering.md#t1) and share anonymized results: which controls were infeasible, ambiguous, redundant or missing.

## Mappings

v1.0 maps controls to the [OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2026](https://github.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/blob/main/2026/README.md), the [OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-for-2026/), [MITRE ATLAS](https://github.com/mitre-atlas/atlas-data/releases/tag/v2026.09) (release 2026.09), [NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-1) and [ISO/IEC 42001:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/42001) Annex A. The following are not yet done.

- **[CSA AI Controls Matrix (AICM)](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/artifacts/ai-controls-matrix-v1-1) v1.1, at control level.** AICM v1.1 (June 2026) has 247 control objectives across 18 domains. A control-level mapping would let organizations that use AICM reuse Ballast assessments. The mapping will cite AICM identifiers only. *Target: v1.1.*
- **[ETSI EN 304 223](https://portal.etsi.org/webapp/WorkProgram/Report_WorkItem.asp?WKI_ID=74952) V2.1.1 (Dec 2025).** Individual provisions may be referenced informatively, but there is no systematic provision-by-provision mapping. *Target: v1.1.*
- **[EU AI Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai) articles.** [BAL-1.12](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-12) records an asset's regulatory classification and hands non-security obligations to a named owner, but no article-level mapping exists. Several articles will map only partially, and some (for example the Art. 50 transparency obligations) will be listed as not addressed. A mapping will not establish compliance. *Target: v1.1.*
- **[OWASP Agentic AI Threats and Mitigations](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/agentic-ai-threats-and-mitigations/) v1.1 (T-numbers).** v1.0 uses the [OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-for-2026/) (ASI01 to ASI10) as its primary agentic reference. A secondary mapping to threats [T1 to T17](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/agentic-ai-threats-and-mitigations/), with a coverage table marking each as addressed, partial or out of scope (for example T15 Human Manipulation), is planned. *Target: v1.1.*
- **Other references not yet mapped at control level:** [NIST AI 600-1](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1) (Generative AI Profile), [ISO/IEC 27001:2022](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001) Annex A for the general controls Ballast assumes, [NIST CSF 2.0](https://www.nist.gov/publications/nist-cybersecurity-framework-csf-20) functions, [NIST AI 100-2e2025](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-2e2025) (Adversarial Machine Learning: A Taxonomy and Terminology of Attacks and Mitigations), [NIST SP 800-218A](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/218/a/final) and [OWASP GenAI Data Security Risks and Mitigations 2026](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-genai-data-security-risks-mitigations-2026/). *Target: v1.1.*
- **Consistency check against OWASP's own cross-mappings.** The LLM Top 10 2026 and the ASI 2026 documents publish their own mappings to each other and to ATLAS and [NIST AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework). Ballast's mappings have not yet been checked against them systematically. *Target: v1.1.*

## Distribution and tooling

- **OSCAL and JSON distribution.** The catalog and control-assessment templates are distributed as CSV and Markdown. An optional [AISMM-based maturity scorecard](guides/maturity/README.md) is also available in Excel for category-level program self-assessment. Planned: a JSON Schema for the catalog and template CSVs, then an OSCAL catalog (one group per domain, with [Control Tier](guides/tiering.md#control-tiers), scope and applies_to as properties) and OSCAL profiles for [T1](guides/tiering.md#t1), [T1](guides/tiering.md#t1) plus [T2](guides/tiering.md#t2) and each example asset type, all generated from the CSV. *Target: v1.1.*
- **Mapping qualifiers and reverse coverage.** v1.0 mappings are unqualified. Planned for v1.1: a primary or contributing qualifier and a short rationale on every row of control-mappings.csv, and a reverse-coverage appendix listing referenced items Ballast does not address. Release checks cover catalog consistency, mapping tables, published control counts and example selections, capability-tag vocabulary and syntax, control IDs, local links and publication fields. Semantic control-dependency checks, including whether a [T1](guides/tiering.md#t1) control depends on a [T2](guides/tiering.md#t2) or [T3](guides/tiering.md#t3) control, remain planned. *Target: v1.1.*

## Guidance

- **Architecture pattern guide.** The controls state outcomes; there is no guide yet showing reference designs that meet them. Planned patterns, each mapped to BAL IDs: Rule of Two patterns and the prompt-injection design patterns of Beurer-Kellner et al. (2025), such as plan-then-execute and dual LLM ([BAL-3.5](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-3-5), [BAL-6.1](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-6-1)); OAuth token exchange ([RFC 8693](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc8693/)) for on-behalf-of delegation ([BAL-2.3](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-2-3), [BAL-2.12](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-2-12)); sandbox plus egress proxy ([BAL-6.13](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-6-13), [BAL-6.14](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-6-14)); a curated MCP server and agent component registry ([BAL-5.16](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-5-16) to [BAL-5.18](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-5-18)); and placement of non-bypassable policy enforcement points in gateways, MCP proxies or platform controls, with SDK middleware as supporting instrumentation ([BAL-3.2](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-3-2), [BAL-6.18](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-6-18)). *Target: v1.1.*
- **Deployment-model responsibility matrix.** v1.0 scopes each control as Program, Vendor or Asset and uses capability tags, but does not yet say, per deployment model (vendor-operated SaaS AI, provider-hosted model APIs, cloud AI platforms, self-hosted), which controls the customer implements, which are shared, and which can only be evidenced from the provider ([BAL-8.6](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-8-6)). *Target: v1.1.*
- **Sector profile for financial services and insurance.** An optional profile, kept outside the core crosswalk, relating Ballast to NYDFS cybersecurity requirements, the [NAIC model bulletin](https://content.naic.org/article/naic-members-approve-model-bulletin-use-ai-insurers) on insurers' use of AI systems, the EU Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) and model-risk management guidance. *Target: v1.1.*
- **Tooling selection criteria.** Ballast controls state outcomes and contain no product-feature requirements. A non-normative guide for evaluating AI security tooling against Ballast outcomes is not yet written. *Target: later.*
- **Formal test procedures.** Each control has evidence examples, but there are no step-by-step assessment procedures yet, and organization-defined parameters (such as "a defined period") have suggested defaults rather than benchmarked values. *Target: v1.1, informed by pilots.*

## Known limitations of the v1.0 controls

- **Prompt injection remains a residual risk.** No control claims to prevent it. [T1](guides/tiering.md#t1) relies on authority set outside the model ([BAL-6.1](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-6-1)), capability separation ([BAL-3.5](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-3-5)) and containment; detection controls such as [BAL-6.4](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-6-4) are tripwires. Published adaptive attacks have bypassed most of the recent defenses they were tested against (Nasr, Carlini, Tramer et al., "The Attacker Moves Second", 2025), so guardrail efficacy ([BAL-5.11](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-5-11)) and adaptive testing ([BAL-5.9](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-5-9)) results should be read as point-in-time measurements.
- **Vendor-operated AI depends on what vendors expose.** For embedded SaaS copilots and vendor-hosted agents, several asset-level controls can be met only through tenant configuration under [BAL-6.17](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-6-17) plus control-specific vendor evidence, reviewed in a structured way under [BAL-8.6](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-8-6) for High and Critical assets (for example [BAL-4.11](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-4-11) output release in embedded copilots). Where a vendor does not expose a needed control or log, adopters will record a gap or an exception ([BAL-1.13](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-13)), except for the non-acceptable controls in the tiering guide, where a vendor gap that leaves the control unmet blocks production use of that asset. We expect this list to shrink as vendors add controls, and we welcome reports of what each platform does and does not support.
- **Delegated identity for endpoint coding agents.** Agents that act through a developer's own git, SSH or cloud CLI credentials usually cannot carry a separate actor identity. v1.0 accepts this for single-user assistants under the [BAL-2.3](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-2-3) carve-out (no credentials beyond the user's, actions recorded as the assistant's), but downstream systems still see only the user. Separate agent identity for coding agents is a pilot question for v1.1.
- **AI SDK vulnerabilities in built agents.** [BAL-6.19](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-6-19) sets a remediation timeline only for INFRA assets and known-exploited vulnerabilities. Advisories with no CVE or KEV entry in MCP and agent SDKs embedded in built agents have no v1.0 timeline ([BAL-1.7](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-7) records the SDKs). *Target: v1.1.*
- **Bulk export by third-party AI integrations** is detected only at [T2](guides/tiering.md#t2) ([BAL-7.5](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-7-5), [BAL-7.12](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-7-12)). *Target: v1.1.*
- **[T3](guides/tiering.md#t3) controls are emerging practice.** [BAL-4.16](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-4-16) (embedding-space attack detection) and [BAL-6.22](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-6-22) (provenance-tracked execution) draw on research-grade techniques, and their assessment criteria are less mature than those of [T1](guides/tiering.md#t1) and [T2](guides/tiering.md#t2).
- **Multi-agent systems.** Agent-to-agent controls ([BAL-2.12](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-2-12), [BAL-6.21](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-6-21)) are [T2](guides/tiering.md#t2) and depend on protocols that are still changing. Ballast does not claim that injection cannot propagate between agents.

## Project governance

- Ballast is currently maintained by its two authors, Raoul Hira and Avishkar (Avi) Nikum. Independent maintainers, a named reviewer list and a possible neutral home for the project are deferred. *Target: after v1.0.*

## Watch list

These are tracked, and mappings or control changes will follow when they stabilize.

- **[NIST AI RMF revision](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework).** NIST states that AI RMF 1.0 is being revised under the July 2025 AI Action Plan. No revised draft had been published as of October 2026. Mappings will be updated on publication.
- **[NIST Cyber SI Profile](https://www.nccoe.nist.gov/projects/cyber-si-profile) project.** Its [NIST IR 8596](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8596/iprd) initial preliminary draft, published as *Cybersecurity Framework Profile for Artificial Intelligence ([Cyber AI Profile](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8596/iprd)): NIST Community Profile* (16 Dec 2025), has three focus areas: Secure, Defend and Thwart. Ballast addresses Secure; a mapping is planned as the profile develops.
- **[NIST SP 800-53 Control Overlays for Super Intelligence Systems (COSIS)](https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/cosis) (formerly COSAiS).** The project was renamed on 20 October 2026; earlier publication titles remain unchanged. A concept paper (August 2025) and predictive-AI discussion outline (January 2026) are available; single-agent and multi-agent overlays are proposed. When published, an SP 800-53 column will be considered for Domains 2, 3, 6 and 7.
- **[ISO/IEC 27090:2026](https://www.iso.org/standard/27090)** (guidance for addressing security threats and failures in AI systems) was published on 14 October 2026. A Ballast mapping is planned.
- **[OWASP MCP Top 10](https://owasp.org/projects/mcp-top-10)** (beta), **[OWASP Non-Human Identities Top 10](https://owasp.org/projects/non-human-identities-top-10)** and **[OWASP Agentic Skills Top 10](https://owasp.org/projects/agentic-skills-top-10)** (1.0-2026, incubator). Candidate mappings for Domains 2, 3, 5, 6 and 8.
- **[OWASP Agent Control Standard](https://genai-security-project.github.io/agent-control-standard/)** (v0.1.0). Defines runtime policy hooks for agent platforms; relevant to [BAL-3.2](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-3-2), [BAL-6.18](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-6-18) and [BAL-7.1](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-7-1).
- **Protocol specifications.** MCP authorization ([BAL-2.8](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-2-8)) and A2A agent descriptors ([BAL-6.21](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-6-21)) are checked against the current specifications at each release.
- **[MITRE ATLAS](https://github.com/mitre-atlas/atlas-data/releases/tag/v2026.09).** ATLAS releases roughly monthly. v1.0 is mapped to release 2026.09 and will be re-validated before each Ballast release.
- **[EU AI Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai) timeline.** Changes to application dates affect the regulatory classification recorded under [BAL-1.12](controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-12).

## How to help

- **Pilot [T1](guides/tiering.md#t1)** on one or more AI assets and report what was infeasible, ambiguous or missing (anonymized is fine).
- **Propose mappings** for any item above, citing identifiers and linking to the source. Do not paste text from OWASP (CC BY-SA) or from ISO or other paywalled standards.
- **Report a gap:** an incident, technique or deployment pattern that no Ballast control addresses, with a reference where possible.
- **Tell us what platforms support:** which controls a given SaaS AI platform, model provider or agent runtime lets customers configure or evidence.

Open an issue as described in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md). Report anything sensitive privately as described in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).
