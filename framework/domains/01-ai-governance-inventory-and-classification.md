# Domain 1: AI Governance, Inventory & Classification

**Objective:** Know every AI asset the organization runs, who owns it, how risky it is and which rules apply to it, and govern each one from intake to retirement under a published policy, clear decision rights and trained people.

## Why it matters

Every other Ballast control attaches to an AI asset that someone owns, has rated and has approved, so governance and inventory come first. AI now arrives through many doors: vendor features switched on inside SaaS, coding agents and extensions developers install, agents built in low-code tools, and model APIs called straight from code. In IBM's 2025 Cost of a Data Breach report, 97% of organizations that reported a breach of an AI model or application lacked proper AI access controls, and 63% of breached organizations had no AI governance policy or were still developing one. In August 2025 the s1ngularity malware turned AI coding CLIs already installed on developer machines into secret-hunting tools, an exposure no one can assess without knowing which agents run where.

**Key risks addressed**

- [OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2026](https://github.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/blob/main/2026/README.md): LLM04:2026 Supply Chain; LLM07:2026 Misinformation
- [OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-for-2026/): ASI03 Identity & Privilege Abuse; ASI04 Agentic Supply Chain Vulnerabilities; ASI09 Human-Agent Trust Exploitation; ASI10 Rogue Agents
- [MITRE ATLAS](https://github.com/mitre-atlas/atlas-data/releases/tag/v2026.09) (release 2026.09): AML.T0010 AI Supply Chain Compromise; AML.T0012 Valid Accounts; AML.T0103 Deploy AI Agent; AML.T0132 Misconfigured or Publicly Exposed AI Services

Control-level references to OWASP, [MITRE ATLAS](https://github.com/mitre-atlas/atlas-data/releases/tag/v2026.09), [NIST AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) and [ISO/IEC 42001](https://www.iso.org/standard/42001): [Standards crosswalk](../../mappings/crosswalk.md#domain-1)

## How this domain fits together

- **Program foundations (once per organization):** policy ([BAL-1.1](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-1)), decision rights ([BAL-1.2](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-2)), training ([BAL-1.3](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-3)), exceptions ([BAL-1.13](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-13)) and, once any High or Critical asset exists, risk reporting ([BAL-1.14](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-14)).
- **Front door:** every asset is registered and approved before it reaches production users or data ([BAL-1.6](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-6)), with an owner ([BAL-1.4](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-4)), a risk rating ([BAL-1.5](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-5)) and a regulatory classification ([BAL-1.12](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-12)). Built assets add an AI bill of materials ([BAL-1.7](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-7)). Citizen-built agents are gated before they are shared ([BAL-1.11](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-11)).
- **Safety net:** discovery finds what bypassed the front door (model and provider usage [BAL-1.8](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-8), endpoint AI [BAL-1.9](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-9), embedded SaaS AI [BAL-1.10](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-10), unsanctioned services [BAL-8.9](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-8-9)) and routes it to intake or to shadow-AI response ([BAL-8.10](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-8-10)).
- **Exit:** retirement removes every residual identity, grant, interface and data store ([BAL-1.15](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-15)).

## Asset risk rating at a glance

[BAL-1.5](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-5) rates each AI asset on five inputs: **reach**, **exposure** (untrusted content and external users), **access** (the most sensitive data and systems it can reach), **agency** (the highest [autonomy level](../../guides/tiering.md#autonomy-levels) per action class, [A0](../../guides/tiering.md#a0)-[A4](../../guides/tiering.md#a4), defined in [Domain 3](03-delegated-authorization-and-least-agency.md)) and **consequential use**.

| Rating | Assigned when | [Control Tiers](../../guides/tiering.md#control-tiers) required |
|---|---|---|
| **High** | Any one of: **TR-A** can reach restricted or regulated data as the organization classifies it (for example health, payment card or special-category personal data, credentials and secrets, privileged systems), not merely internal or client-confidential data; **TR-B** any action class at [A3](../../guides/tiering.md#a3) or above, or carries the EXEC tag; **TR-C** the untrusted-input combination of [BAL-3.5](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-3-5) (untrusted input + sensitive access + an external action or communication channel, including rendering), or external users (PUBLIC); **TR-D** makes or materially supports consequential decisions about individuals, or is high-risk under applicable AI law | [T1](../../guides/tiering.md#t1) + [T2](../../guides/tiering.md#t2) |
| **Critical** | Any action class is at [A4](../../guides/tiering.md#a4); or TR-D holds together with TR-B or TR-C | [T1](../../guides/tiering.md#t1) + [T2](../../guides/tiering.md#t2); [T3](../../guides/tiering.md#t3) recommended, and required where any action class is at [A4](../../guides/tiering.md#a4) |
| **Medium / Low** | None of the above; the split between them is organization-defined | [T1](../../guides/tiering.md#t1) |

The rating also sets the approval route ([BAL-1.2](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-2), [BAL-1.6](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-6)) and review cadence, and is re-assessed on material change. Event-triggered initiation by external content counts as untrusted input. [A4](../../guides/tiering.md#a4) is permitted only by authorization ([BAL-1.13](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-13), [BAL-3.1](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-3-1)). The full rubric is in [Control Tiers](../../guides/tiering.md).

Inventory completeness can be measured as the share of assets found by discovery ([BAL-1.8](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-8), [1.9](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-9), [1.10](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-10), [8.9](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-8-9)) that were already registered. Useful additions: approval volume and time-to-decide ([BAL-3.12](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-3-12)) and [A3](../../guides/tiering.md#a3)-or-higher agents with a tested kill switch ([BAL-3.7](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-3-7)). |
| [BAL-1.15](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-15) | AI asset decommissioning | Retirement often leaves access behind: Google Cloud documents that deleting an Agent Engine agent does not remove IAM bindings that reference its principal; they must be removed manually. Agent identities retire through [BAL-2.2](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-2-2), third-party AI grants through [BAL-2.7](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-2-7), and legal hold overrides deletion ([BAL-4.4](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-4-4)). Triggers, schedules and sub-agents recorded under [BAL-3.7](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-3-7) show what else must be switched off. |
