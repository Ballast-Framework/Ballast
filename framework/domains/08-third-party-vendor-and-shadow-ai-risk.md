# Domain 8: Third-Party, Vendor & Shadow AI Risk

**Objective:** Assess externally sourced models before use, hold AI vendors and suppliers to AI-specific commitments that stay current as they change, and steer unsanctioned AI use to approved alternatives.

## Why it matters

Most enterprise AI runs inside someone else's service, under terms that can change after signature. In 2025 the ShadowLeak attack took Gmail data through ChatGPT Deep Research directly from the vendor's cloud, invisible to enterprise egress controls and logs. The same year a bug in Asana's MCP server exposed data from about 1,000 customers to users in other organizations. By September 2026 Microsoft documented Anthropic models as a default-on sub-processor for most commercial tenants outside the EU, EFTA and UK, excluded from its EU Data Boundary. Employees also bring consumer terms to work through personal AI accounts. This domain makes vendor commitments AI-specific and keeps them current, assesses external models before use, extends AI terms to ordinary suppliers, and steers unsanctioned use to approved alternatives rather than only blocking it.

**Key risks addressed**

- [OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2026](https://github.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10/blob/main/2026/README.md): LLM04:2026 Supply Chain; LLM02:2026 Sensitive Information Disclosure; LLM05:2026 Data and Model Poisoning; LLM01:2026 Prompt Injection; LLM10:2026 Improper Output Handling
- [OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-for-2026/): ASI04 Agentic Supply Chain Vulnerabilities; ASI01 Agent Goal Hijack; ASI02 Tool Misuse & Exploitation
- [MITRE ATLAS](https://github.com/mitre-atlas/atlas-data/releases/tag/v2026.09) (release 2026.09): AML.T0010.003 AI Supply Chain Compromise: Model; AML.T0115.001 Publish Poisoned AI Artifacts: Models; AML.T0111 AI Supply Chain Reputation Inflation; AML.T0057 LLM Data Leakage; AML.T0051.001 LLM Prompt Injection: Indirect; AML.T0077 LLM Response Rendering; AML.T0086 Exfiltration via AI Agent Tool Invocation; AML.T0114 AI Service Web Interface

Control-level references to OWASP, [MITRE ATLAS](https://github.com/mitre-atlas/atlas-data/releases/tag/v2026.09), [NIST AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) and [ISO/IEC 42001](https://www.iso.org/standard/42001): [Standards crosswalk](../../mappings/crosswalk.md#domain-8)

## Shared responsibility for vendor-operated AI

Sometimes the enforcement point a control requires sits inside a vendor's service: output rendering, server-side egress, injection defenses, the approval prompt or the agent action log. The control still applies to the asset. It is not marked Not applicable, because Not applicable comes only from capability tags. Evidence comes in two layers:

1. **Tenant configuration.** Every setting the vendor exposes for that enforcement point is set and held to the approved baseline ([BAL-6.17](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-6-17)). Examples are allowed-URL lists, connector and outbound-action settings, memory and audit-log export.
2. **Provider evidence specific to the control.** Vendor documentation, the scope of the vendor's independent assurance ([BAL-8.5](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-8-5)) or the organization's own test shows that the vendor-side mechanism exists and covers the features in use. For assets rated High or Critical, [BAL-8.6](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-8-6) adds a structured, repeated review of the vendor's injection and exfiltration defenses.

| Enforcement point inside the vendor service | Control | Typical evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Rendering of links, images and previews | [BAL-6.3](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-6-3) | Rendering and allowed-URL settings; vendor rendering documentation |
| Separation of untrusted input, sensitive access and outbound channels | [BAL-3.5](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-3-5) | A/B/C profile of the features enabled; vendor documentation of trust separation |
| Approval of high-risk actions | [BAL-3.6](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-3-6) | Approval settings per action class; vendor documentation of what the approval prompt shows |
| Injection detection for agents the organization builds on a vendor platform | [BAL-6.4](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-6-4) | Platform guardrail settings; detections forwarded to monitoring ([BAL-7.3](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-7-3)) |
| Agent action log | [BAL-7.1](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-7-1) | Vendor audit log enabled and exported to the organization's monitoring |
| Halting a vendor-hosted agent | [BAL-3.7](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-3-7) | Tested admin disable control, or a contractual commitment |

Should neither layer seal a gap, the gap receives a compensating control or is permitted via [BAL-1.13](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-13). Instances of compensating controls include restricting the allowed data classes ([BAL-8.7](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-8-7)) or deactivating the feature through the tenant baseline ([BAL-6.17](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-6-17)).

## How the vendor and shadow AI controls connect

- **Models and contracts.** [BAL-8.1](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-8-1) assesses externally sourced models before ingestion. [BAL-8.2](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-8-2) to [8.5](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-8-5) set AI-specific terms and assurance for AI vendors. [BAL-8.12](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-8-12) extends the data-use terms to ordinary suppliers that apply AI to restricted or regulated data.
- **Profile, publish, keep current.** [BAL-8.7](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-8-7) profiles each vendor, plan and model. [BAL-8.10](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-8-10) turns the profiles into the approved catalog that the AI policy ([BAL-1.1](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-1)) references. [BAL-8.8](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-8-8) keeps both current as vendors change.
- **Prevent, detect, redirect.** [BAL-8.9](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-8-9) prevents or detects unsanctioned use, and [BAL-8.10](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-8-10) responds with an approved alternative. [BAL-8.11](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-8-11) ([T2](../../guides/tiering.md#t2)) adds detection of personal-license agents from the audit logs of the resources they touch.
- **Continuity.** [BAL-8.13](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-8-13) ([T1](../../guides/tiering.md#t1)) gives business-critical AI assets a tested fallback for provider outage, model retirement or a forced change of terms, regardless of their risk rating. A manual process may be sufficient if it meets the recovery objective.
- **Related controls in other domains.** Provider account and API key inventory ([BAL-1.8](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-8)); endpoint AI discovery ([BAL-1.9](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-9)); embedded SaaS AI inventory ([BAL-1.10](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-10)); consent to third-party AI apps and their grants ([BAL-2.6](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-2-6), [2.7](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-2-7)); DLP coverage of AI channels ([BAL-4.5](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-4-5)); destination-aware input protection using the [BAL-8.7](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-8-7) profiles ([BAL-4.6](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-4-6)); personal-license coding tools ([BAL-5.21](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-5-21)); AI browsers blocked by default ([BAL-6.20](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-6-20)). Meeting notetakers and other ambient-capture AI are governed by the AI policy ([BAL-1.1](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-1-1)), the tenant baseline ([BAL-6.17](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-6-17)) and app consent ([BAL-2.6](../../controls/control-catalog.md#bal-2-6)).
